Tip: superheat (HVAC School)

Superheat is how far the suction vapor is above its boiling (dew) point. HVAC School's rule of thumb for a TXV system is about 10°F, give or take 5. For a fixed orifice, use the manufacturer's chart: HVAC School gives no single number for it.

Tip: subcooling (HVAC School)

Subcooling is how far the liquid is below its bubble point. HVAC School's rule of thumb for TXV systems is about 8–14°F. Always check the nameplate target first.

How these are worked out

Moist-air numbers (wet bulb, dew point, humidity ratio, enthalpy) use the standard ASHRAE equations at the atmospheric pressure from the Profile page. Capacity uses the common field formulas for standard air: sensible = 1.08 × CFM × temperature split; total = 4.5 × CFM × enthalpy drop; latent = total − sensible; each corrected for the typed atmospheric pressure. They are estimates only as good as your airflow and humidity readings.

Tip: temperature split and airflow (HVAC School)

HVAC School gives 16–22°F as the usual air temperature split at about 400 CFM per ton, and says about 350 CFM per ton is more common today. Total external static is usually 0.3–0.7 in. WC. The ranges here are the ones YOU type: the page does not fill them in.

How these are worked out

Watts = volts × amps × power factor (× 1.732 for a three-phase unit, picked per unit). kVA = volts × amps. Efficiency uses the total capacity from the Airflow page: EER = total BTU/h ÷ total watts of both units; kW per ton = total kW ÷ (total BTU/h ÷ 12,000); COP = EER ÷ 3.412. Field estimates only: the answer is only as good as the airflow, humidity and meter readings. Amps words use our own bands: IN RANGE up to 100% of rated, yellow up to 115%. Volts: IN RANGE 90–110% of rated, yellow within 5% of that. These bands are not from MeasureQuick or HVAC School.
