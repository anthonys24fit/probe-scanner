Flag limits and checks

A black flag means a reading that could lead to system or compressor failure if it is left unresolved (that is how measureQuick describes it). The page uses automatic limits: a discharge line hotter than 225°F, or a compression ratio of 11 to 1 or higher. Type your own below only to replace them. There is no automatic suction-line limit because no published number was found.

Where the automatic limits come from

These numbers come from web articles and maker notes for particular compressors, found on 2026-11-16. The automatic limits are starting points, not your compressor’s own data: if your compressor maker publishes a different limit, type it above.

Discharge line: one trade article puts the limit at about 225°F, measured about 3 inches from the compressor; a Copeland knowledge-base entry gives 240°F as the maximum external discharge line temperature for one older scroll model; newer Copeland guides list model-specific discharge gas limits of about 248 to 284°F (120 to 140°C). Limits differ by compressor, so use the compressor maker's number. Compression ratio and suction line: no single published limit was found; check the compressor maker's application guide.

Tip: superheat (HVAC School)

Superheat is how far the suction vapor is above its boiling (dew) point. HVAC School's rule of thumb for a TXV system is about 10°F, give or take 5. For a fixed metering device (piston or capillary tube), use the manufacturer's chart: HVAC School gives no single number for it.

Read more at HVAC School

Tip: subcooling (HVAC School)

Subcooling is how far the liquid is below its bubble point. HVAC School's rule of thumb for TXV systems is about 8–14°F. Always check the nameplate target first.

Read more at HVAC School

How these are worked out

Moist-air numbers (wet bulb, dew point, humidity ratio, enthalpy) use the standard ASHRAE equations at the atmospheric pressure from the Job tab. Capacity uses the common field formulas for standard air: sensible = 1.08 × CFM × temperature split; total = 4.5 × CFM × enthalpy drop; latent = total − sensible; each corrected for the typed atmospheric pressure. They are estimates only as good as your airflow and humidity readings.

Tip: temperature split and airflow (HVAC School)

HVAC School gives 16–22°F as the usual air temperature split at about 400 CFM per ton, and says about 350 CFM per ton is more common today. Total external static is usually 0.3–0.7 in. WC. The ranges used here are the ones YOU type: the page does not fill them in.

Electrical & efficiency

How these are worked out

Watts = volts × amps × power factor (× 1.732 for a three-phase unit, picked per unit). kVA = volts × amps. Efficiency uses the total capacity from the Airflow page: EER = total BTU/h ÷ total watts of both units; kW per ton = total kW ÷ (total BTU/h ÷ 12,000); COP = EER ÷ 3.412. Field estimates only: the answer is only as good as the airflow, humidity and meter readings. Amps words use our own bands: IN RANGE up to 100% of rated, yellow up to 115%. Volts: IN RANGE 90–110% of rated, yellow within 5% of that. These bands are not from MeasureQuick or HVAC School.
